Weddings

Weddings & receptions

The gap between the ceremony and dinner is where receptions lose momentum. That is the slot the range fills. We set up before guests arrive and open the lanes when the cocktail hour starts, so there is something to do that isn't standing near the bar.

It plays well with the crowd that doesn't dance. Grandparents watch, groomsmen get competitive, and the photographer usually ends up with the best candid shots of the night. Most valley weddings we work take the trailer range if there is a lot or a field to park it on, and the freestanding targets if the venue is tight.

Practical notes: we work around your timeline, not through it. Tell us when the toasts are and we will pause. If the reception runs past dark, the lanes are lit.

Corporate

Corporate events & team building

Team building usually means an activity nobody is good at, which is exactly why axe throwing works — the org chart stops mattering about four minutes in. It is also one of the few group activities where the people who opt out still have a good time watching.

We run these as open lanes rather than a structured tournament by default, but a bracket is easy if you want one — it gives the event a shape and an ending. Company parties, summer picnics, sales kickoffs, retreats at valley properties, client appreciation days.

Corporate events are also the ones that run outside the summer wedding rush, so weekday and off-season dates are usually wide open.

Everything Else

Parties, milestones & community events

Bachelor & bachelorette parties

The group is already together and already looking for something to do between dinner and whatever comes next. Freestanding targets at the rental house is the usual call — no one has to drive.

Birthdays, graduations & reunions

Mixed ages, mixed enthusiasm, and someone always surprises themselves. Works in a backyard, a park pavilion, or a rented hall lot. Two hours is usually plenty.

Festivals & fundraisers

The trailer range is a draw on its own — people walk over to see what it is. Good fit for community days, brewery events, rodeo grounds and school fundraisers where you need a booth that pulls a crowd.

Logistics

What we need from you

Short list. Most venues clear it without thinking about it.

If you want the trailer range

Room to park

  • A reasonably level spot a truck can back into
  • Lot, field, wide driveway or paved area all work
  • Nothing gets staked into the ground
  • Covered, so it keeps running if the weather turns
If you want freestanding targets

About 20 x 30 feet

  • Grass, gravel or pavement — all fine
  • Clear space behind and to the sides of the lanes
  • Nothing attached to a building or fence
  • Outdoor only, so we'll agree a weather plan

No power or water needed. We bring targets, axes, safety setup, lighting and a coach who runs the lanes the whole time. Every thrower signs a waiver before they pick up an axe.

Planning Questions

The ones we get asked most

How long should we book for?

Two hours is the minimum and covers groups up to about 30. Past that — or if you want the lanes open the whole event rather than a set window — three to four hours keeps the line moving.

How many people can throw at once?

Guests rotate through in small groups with a coach. Everyone else watches from close up, which is most of the fun. Give us your headcount and we'll tell you how long you actually need.

Can guests drink?

Alcohol is normal at the weddings and parties we work. The coach controls the lanes and will turn away anyone who shouldn't be throwing. Everyone signs a waiver first.

What if it rains?

The trailer range is covered and keeps going. The freestanding targets are outdoor-only, so for those we agree a plan with you ahead of time.

Is it safe around kids?

A coach controls the throwing area the entire event and spectators stay behind the line. Age limits depend on the group and the event — raise it when you reach out and we'll talk it through.

Do you travel outside the Grand Valley?

Constantly. Most of our summer is up the Roaring Fork Valley. Travel is folded into the quote rather than billed separately.
